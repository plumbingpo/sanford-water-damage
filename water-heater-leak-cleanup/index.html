Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Sanford

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

In Sanford, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Platform and Subfloor

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.
