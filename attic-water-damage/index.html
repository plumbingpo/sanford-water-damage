Attic Water Damage: What Sanford Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. Our team in Sanford inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • Bubbling or peeling ceiling paint in a room on the top floor

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

A Falling Ceiling

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Mold on Sheathing

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Wet Wiring

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.
