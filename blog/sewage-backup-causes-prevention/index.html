Why Sewage Backups Happen in Sanford, FL

Home / Blog / Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Sanford, FL

For a homeowner, a backed-up sewer line ranks near the top of the list of unpleasant emergencies. Raw sewage entering your living spaces creates immediate health hazards, causes extensive property damage, and requires professional cleanup that can cost thousands of dollars. Your risk drops significantly when you know what causes backups and take steps to prevent them. This guide from Sanford Restoration Co covers the common causes of sewage backups in Sanford, FL and practical steps you can take to protect your property.

What Causes a Sewer to Back Up

A backup happens when wastewater leaving your property cannot reach the septic tank or the municipal sewer system the way it should. Usual culprits are tree roots growing into sewer pipes; lines clogged by a buildup of hair, grease, and debris; pipes that are old or have collapsed; combined sewer overflow events; an overloaded municipal system in heavy rain; and toilets used to flush the wrong things, such as feminine products, wipes, and too much paper.

How Tree Roots Block Sewer Pipes

Roots from nearby trees are a leading reason sewer lines in Sanford get blocked. Because roots grow toward water, a pipe that always carries it draws them in. They work their way in at cracks, joints, and connection points, then slowly fill the inside of the pipe until it blocks and backs up. Older sewer lines made of clay tile are especially easy for roots to invade. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

How to Prevent a Sewage Backup

Several habits and upgrades help keep sewage out of a Sanford home, and the first is a backwater valve on the sewer connection, which stops backflow from the municipal system. Grease and cooking oil should never go down a drain. Do not flush feminine products, wipes, or anything other than toilet paper. Routine inspection and cleaning of your sewer line should be on the calendar. Deal with a slow drain quickly, before it turns into a full blockage. Know the location of your sewer cleanout for emergency access. It may also be worth looking at sewer line coverage or a backup endorsement for your homeowner's insurance.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

When sewage backs up, leave the cleanup alone. Sewage contains dangerous pathogens including bacteria, viruses, and parasites that can cause serious illness.
