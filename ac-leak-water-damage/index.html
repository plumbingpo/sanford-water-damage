AC Leak Water Damage Help in Sanford

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

The Platform and Closet Walls

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Mold Near the Return

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Trim and Baseboards

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

What We Do

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Measure the Spread

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
